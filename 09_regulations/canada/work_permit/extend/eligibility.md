# Who can extend

## Who can apply

This application is for you if you

- want to extend your work permit
- need to make changes to the conditions on your work permit, such as
  - the type of work you can do
  - the employer you can work for
  - where you can work
    - This includes restrictions because [you needed a medical exam](/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/application/medical-police/medical-exams/requirements-temporary-residents.html#needs). For instance, you received an open work permit with restrictions on working in health care because you hadn’t completed a medical exam.
    - You can [remove the conditions](https://www.ircc.canada.ca/english/helpcentre/answer.asp?qnum=1647&top=17) on where you can work once you get a medical exam.
- need to change your gender on your work permit
  - **If you identify as a different gender** than the one on your work permit, use this application and include the [Request for a Change of Sex or Gender Identifier form [IRM 0002] (PDF, 1.47 MB)](/content/dam/ircc/documents/pdf/english/kits/forms/irm0002e.pdf).
  - **If we made a mistake** on your work permit, you can [ask to amend a valid temporary resident document](/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/application/application-forms-guides/guide-5218-request-amend-record-landing-confirmation-permanent-residence-valid-temporary-resident-documents.html).

If you don’t have a work permit but want to work in Canada temporarily, [find out what you need to do](/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/work-canada.html#location).

### Employer-specific work permits

You need to apply to extend or change the conditions of your work permit if:

- your job is extended
- there are changes to your current job, such as:
  - a change in your pay
  - new responsibilities
- you’re offered a different job
- you want to work for a new employer

You can’t start working at a different job or for a new employer until you get your work permit with the new conditions.

#### Work permits for Quebec skilled workers who applied for the Programme de sélection des travailleurs qualifiés New

You may be eligible for an employer-specific work permit to keep working in Quebec for up to 12 months if you

- submitted a demande de sélection permanente to Quebec for an application under the [Programme de sélection des travailleurs qualifiés](https://www.quebec.ca/en/immigration/permanent/skilled-workers/skilled-worker-selection-program)
- currently hold, or recently held an work permit requiring a labour market impact assessment for a job in Quebec
- will work for the same employer in Quebec

[Find out more about this work permit](/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/work-canada/special-instructions/work-permit-skilled-workers-quebec-pstq.html).

### Open work permits

If you have an open work permit, you may be able to extend it as long as you’re still [eligible to work in Canada with an open work permit](#centred-popup10).

#### Who can apply for an open work permit?

You may be eligible for an [open work permit](http://www.ircc.canada.ca/english/helpcentre/answer.asp?qnum=176&top=17) if you:

- are an international student who graduated from a [designated learning institution](/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/work/after-graduation/eligibility.html) and are eligible for the [Post-Graduation Work Permit Program](/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/work/after-graduation.html)
- are a student who’s no longer able to meet the costs of your studies (destitute student)
- have an employer-specific work permit and are [being abused or at risk of being abused in relation to your job](/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/work-canada/special-instructions/vulnerable-workers.html) in Canada
- applied for permanent residence in Canada
- are a dependent [family member](/en/services/immigration-citizenship/helpcentre/glossary.html#family_members) of someone who applied for permanent residence
- are the spouse or common-law partner of a skilled worker or international student
- are the spouse or common-law partner of an applicant of the [Atlantic Immigration Pilot Program](/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/atlantic-immigration-pilot.html)
- are a refugee, refugee claimant, protected person or their family member
- are under an unenforceable removal order
- are a temporary resident permit holder
- are a young worker participating in special programs
