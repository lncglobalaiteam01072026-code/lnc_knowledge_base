# Participate in the National Occupational Classification’s consultations
## Introduction
Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC) and Statistics Canada invite you to provide comments on the National Occupational Classification (NOC).
You can provide comments on the NOC 2021. Your comments and suggestions will be taken into account for the annual Evergreening process of the NOC or for the next major revision if they relate to the structure of the classification or to the composition of the different unit groups.
## Objective
The comments received will be used to support occupational research and to update the NOC.
## Who can participate
This consultation targets:
  * government, all levels;
  * non-governmental organizations;
  * companies and industry associations;
  * academics and researchers;
  * career counselors and career planning specialists; and
  * any other interested person.


## How to participate
Participants are invited to provide feedback via the Contact us section of the website. 
## Consultation period
Stakeholders are invited to submit proposals to review the National Occupational Classification on an ongoing basis. 
Proposals should clearly identify the proposed change to the classification, outline the rationale for the change and include supporting information such as main duties; educational background and certification (if any) and current job titles used in the labour market.
For the upcoming release of NOC 2026, a consensus was reached between Statistics Canada and Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC) to introduce structural changes to the classification. This marks the first time that structural changes have been introduced in two consecutive NOC revisions (five years apart), whereas such changes were previously made only every second revision (ten years apart).
Since April 2024, both departments have a permanent consultation process for the NOC. Proposals for changes can now be submitted and reviewed on an ongoing basis, with a posted cut-off date indicating the deadline for inclusion in a given revision cycle. For NOC 2026 Version 1.0, the cut-off date was November 15, 2024.
Since the implementation of NOC 2021, over 200 recommendations have been received. Upon review of the submissions received, changes are made when supported by research and agreed upon by Statistics Canada for inclusion in the new version. Recommendations for which a decision could not be made as part of this revision cycle will be considered in the next revision cycle.
Key areas targeted by recommendations include:
  * Updates to job titles, including revisions, additions, and reclassifications such as:
    * Indigenous job titles in various occupations across the NOC
    * NOC 21203 – Land surveyors
    * NOC 22114 – Landscape and horticulture technicians and specialists
  * Review of employment requirements for select occupations, such as:
    * NOC 11100 – Financial auditors and accountants
    * NOC 21200 – Architects
    * NOC 31202 – Physiotherapists
  * Clarifications to the duties of the various subgroups within occupations, such as:
    * NOC 14301 – Correspondence, publication and regulatory clerks
    * NOC 21211 – Data scientists
    * NOC 84111 – Silviculture and forestry workers
  * Enhanced classification of occupations within the NOC, such as:
    * NOC 14404 – Dispatchers
    * NOC 41409 – Other professional occupations in social science
    * NOC 42202 – Early childhood educators and assistants
    * NOC 64300 – Maîtres d'hôtel and hosts/hostesses


A total of 165 unit groups will be impacted, including 24 that will undergo structural changes. The updated classification is scheduled for release in December 2026.
## Treatment of comments
A team of representatives from ESDC and Statistics Canada will analyze the comments received. Departments reserve the right to use independent consultants or government employees, if deemed necessary, to assess comments.
If deemed appropriate, departments will contact respondents to ask additional questions or for clarification.
## Official languages
Comments may be written in either of Canada's official languages, English or French.
## Confidentiality
Personal information collected during this consultation will be protected under the [Privacy Act](http://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-21/).
